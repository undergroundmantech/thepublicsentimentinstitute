# Vivek Ramaswamy Watchdog File

**Subject:** Vivek Ramaswamy, Republican nominee for Governor of Ohio, 2026, running with Senate President Rob McColley
**Compiled:** October 23, 2026 for the On Point Politics watchdog database, onpointpolitics.us
**Data cutoff:** October 20, 2026. Latest state figures are the July, August and September monthly reports described on October 13, 2026.
**Next scheduled data drop:** Ohio pre-general and post-general campaign reports and the V-PAC federal pre-general and post-general reports. Exact due dates were not verified in this pass.

## Bottom line up front

Ramaswamy is the most expensive candidate in Ohio history and the money comes from three places: his own fortune, a federal super PAC fed by a handful of billionaires, and a donor base that is broad but thin in Ohio. He has committed more than $36 million of his own money as loans he can repay from donor funds, per the Ohio Capital Journal on October 13, 2026, https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/2026/10/13/ramaswamy-loans-campaign-5-million-more-as-acton-outpaces-him-with-donors/. V-PAC: Victors Not Victims, FEC ID C00892919, has taken at least $30 million from Jeff Yass alone, per Signal Ohio on August 27, 2026, https://signalohio.org/billionaire-jeff-yass-ohio-governor-race-november-election-2026/.

Four interests have both real money in and a dated decision on the next governor's desk:

1. **Billionaire tax cut donors.** Yass, Elon Musk, Ratmir Timashev, Ross Stevens and Bill Ackman want his income tax phase out, which starts with repealing the capital gains tax. That fight is the 2027 budget.
2. **Data center and power interests.** He has promised a day one order halting new data center approvals, which cuts against some V-PAC donors. AEP's PAC and the submetering industry gave to his campaign. He controls PUCO and Power Siting Board seats.
3. **School choice.** Yass and Pansophic Learning want EdChoice protected while hundreds of school districts sue over it. The voucher line was $2.5 billion in the last budget.
4. **Gambling, cannabis and crypto.** The Sports Betting Alliance put $150,000 into V-PAC, and cannabis and crypto executives gave to the campaign. He controls Casino Control Commission seats and cannabis regulators. No public position from him was found on either.

## Committee structure and treasurer

The candidate committee runs the Ramaswamy and McColley ticket. Its press contact is vivekforohio.com. Its Ohio Secretary of State ID and treasurer were not verified in this pass. Strategist Jai Chabria and campaign manager Jonathan Ewing are on record speaking for it, per the Journal-News and the Ohio Capital Journal of August 5, 2025. The aligned vehicle is V-PAC: Victors Not Victims, a federal super PAC. Its chief strategist is Andy Surbian, per Signal Ohio on July 30, 2025, https://signalohio.org/billionaire-makes-huge-contribution-to-political-group-backing-ohio-gop-candidate-vivek-ramaswamy/. Its treasurer was not identified.

Scale. The campaign raised about $19.8 million from donors through December 31, 2025. NBC4 reported more than $50 million raised since he declared, counting his own money, through April 15, 2026, https://www.nbc4i.com/news/your-local-election-hq/acton-ramaswamy-each-raise-over-5-million-from-donors-in-past-four-months/amp/. Donors then gave nearly $4 million in July, about $2.6 million in August and just over $3 million in September 2026. Amy Acton outraised him in each of those months.

Transparency USA's aggregate of state filings shows $25,555,956 in contributions and $19,695,657 in expenditures, https://www.transparencyusa.org/oh/candidate/vivek-ramaswamy. The page shows no date range, so treat it as a cumulative snapshot fetched October 23, 2026.

V-PAC had raised $29.5 million by March 31, 2026 and held $23 million in cash. It had spent $44.6 million on advertising by its latest report, per the Ohio Capital Journal through States Newsroom on October 20, 2026. Axios put spending or reservations by Ramaswamy and allied groups at over $97 million in TV and radio as of mid October.

## Billionaire megadonors

Documented. Jeff Yass gave V-PAC $10 million in March 2025, per Signal Ohio. He added two $5 million checks in the first quarter of 2026, which brought him to $20 million, per the Ohio Capital Journal on April 27, 2026, https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/2026/04/27/ohio-republican-vivek-ramaswamy-holds-big-cash-lead-over-democrat-amy-acton-after-25-million-loan/. Politico then reported a further gift of at least $10 million in late August 2026, as relayed by Signal Ohio. That gift is provisional until V-PAC's federal filing shows it.

Ross Stevens gave $5 million in the first half of 2025 and $1 million in May 2026. Elon Musk gave $5 million in May 2026, Ratmir Timashev gave $5 million in May 2026 and Bill Ackman gave $1 million in June 2026, per the Ohio Capital Journal on July 22, 2026, https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/2026/07/22/elon-musk-3-other-billionaires-put-12-million-behind-pro-ramaswamy-super-pac-in-ohio-governor-race/. That outlet counted $37 million from four billionaires, more than 88 percent of V-PAC's money. V-PAC then spent over $8.1 million in June on an ad against Acton.

Hop two. Spectrum News reported on October 16, 2026 that Jeff and Janine Yass gave $110 million to Republicans this cycle, https://spectrumlocalnews.com/us/snplus/politics/2026/10/16/midterm-elections-top-50-donors-decision-2026. The same report says Musk put $158 million into a Republican super PAC that moved the same amount to a group backing Ken Paxton in Texas. It cites the Washington Post for a Musk cycle total of $369 million. Signal Ohio describes Yass as among the country's biggest backers of taxpayer funded vouchers.

What they want, and the lever. Ramaswamy pledges to phase out the state income tax, starting by eliminating capital gains taxes, per his plan page, https://vivekforohio.com/the-plan/. Innovation Ohio relays a Legislative Service Commission estimate that capital gains repeal alone would cost up to $646 million in 2027. That is an advocacy source and is marked Secondary. At the Columbus Metropolitan Club on October 14, 2026 he also promised to roll property taxes back to pre-pandemic levels at about $4 billion, paid by the state, per the Ohio Capital Journal, https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/2026/10/15/ramaswamy-discusses-death-and-taxes-in-ohio-at-columbus-metropolitan-club/.

Inference: The donors who fund V-PAC are the people who benefit most from removing capital gains taxes. The first executive budget of 2027 is where that request either becomes law or does not. No document shows any donor asking for it.

## Self funding and the jet

Documented. Ramaswamy lent the campaign $230,000 at the end of June 2025 to cover use of his plane. He then lent $25 million that was reported in the first quarter of 2026, and more than $5 million in August and September 2026. All of it is structured as loans. The plane is a 2011 Cessna Citation X owned by V Leasing LLC, whose corporate address matches a home he owns, per Signal Ohio on August 1, 2025, https://signalohio.org/vivek-ramaswamy-uses-his-private-jet-to-campaign-for-ohio-governor/.

Transparency USA shows $519,794.86 paid to V Leasing. Innovation Ohio counted at least $555,676.15 in aircraft spending across V Leasing and five charter firms from July to December 2025. The campaign said donors do not pay for the flights and that his loans document his reimbursement. An election lawyer quoted by Signal said Ohio's ban on direct corporate contributions explains why the plane is leased at market rate from his own company.

Inference: Donor money and his own loan money mix in one account, and the loans can be repaid. So the claim that donors do not pay for the jet depends on how the loans are repaid after the election. The post-general report is the place to check that.

## Party and legislative leadership

Documented. The Ohio Republican Party's state account is the top contributor on Transparency USA at $409,559.53. McColley's own campaign fund moved $290,178.79, which the Ohio Capital Journal also reported as about $290,000 in April 2026. Governor Mike DeWine endorsed Ramaswamy, per the Ohio Capital Journal on May 26, 2026. Secretary of State Frank LaRose has also endorsed him.

The submetering bill shows how this bloc connects to money. Nationwide Energy Partners chief executive Michael DeAscentis II gave to Ramaswamy on June 5, 2026, per the Ohio Capital Journal on June 26, 2026. The amount is printed in garbled form there. Innovation Ohio counts $51,615 from the DeAscentis household across Ramaswamy, Speaker Matt Huffman and Senate utilities chair Shane Wilkin over four days. It says the Senate committee moved HB 173 about two days later. HB 173 would have undone an April 2026 Ohio Supreme Court ruling against submetering. The legislature passed it on roughly party lines, and DeWine vetoed it on June 24, 2026, calling the model fundamentally flawed.

Inference: McColley led the chamber that passed HB 173 while that industry's donors were funding his ticket. A Governor Ramaswamy would be the one deciding whether a refiled bill becomes law. He has said nothing about it.

## Own finances and disclosure history

Forbes put his net worth at about $1.8 billion on November 20, 2025, and his spokesperson called the figure broadly correct, https://www.forbes.com/sites/kylemullins/2025/11/20/vivek-ramaswamys-net-worth-has-nearly-doubled-while-hes-running-for-ohio-governor/. Forbes counted roughly 49 million Roivant shares worth about $1 billion and options worth about $210 million. It put his Strive stake near $150 million and his stake in Chapter, a Medicare startup, near $100 million.

Strive pivoted to a bitcoin treasury company in 2025. Innovation Ohio cites a September 12, 2025 shareholder agreement that treats Strive as a controlled company in which he holds the largest stake. He has said he stepped back from the company. Ohio Ethics Commission disclosure filings were not retrieved in this pass.

## Enforcement and controversies

1. **Credit card reporting.** The campaign had run $509,473 through a campaign card by June 2026, reported as lump sum reimbursements without payees, per the Ohio Capital Journal on June 24, 2026. State Senator Kent Smith complained. The Election Integrity Unit recommended no violation in letters dated September 16, 2026, and Secretary LaRose, who endorsed Ramaswamy, adopted that finding. The campaign did not comment. Smith had not appealed as of October 7, 2026.
2. **Jet payments.** No formal complaint was found. The campaign's response is recorded above.
3. **DHS contract allegation, contested.** Innovation Ohio alleged in March 2026 that money from a $143 million Department of Homeland Security contract reached Ohio consultants tied to Ramaswamy. It named The Strategy Group, which the campaign paid nearly $7 million in the first quarter of 2026, and former spokesperson Tricia McLaughlin. Both posted denials on March 4, 2026, and Ramaswamy did not comment. No primary record was read, so this stays Contested.
4. **Attack ad.** Ramaswamy said on October 20, 2026 that an undisclosed ad attacking Acton is his. A headline in the Ohio Capital Journal on October 22, 2026 reports that DeWine asked him to take it down. The ad's sponsor and buy were not disclosed.
