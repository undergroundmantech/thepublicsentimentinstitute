# Sherrod Brown Watchdog File

**Subject:** Sherrod Brown, Democrat, former three term U.S. senator, nominee in Ohio's 2026 U.S. Senate special election against appointed Sen. Jon Husted
**Compiled:** October 25, 2026 for the On Point Politics watchdog database, onpointpolitics.us
**Data cutoff:** FEC data through October 14, 2026 as compiled by VoteLog; independent expenditures through October 23, 2026.
**Next scheduled data drop:** FEC 48 hour reports through Election Day and the post-general report due December 3, 2026.

## Bottom line up front

Brown is the best funded Senate challenger in this set. VoteLog shows $68,409,200 raised through October 14, 2026, with $57,861,942 from individuals and only $1,055,783 from PACs, https://votelog.org/investigations/ohio-senate-general-money. His campaign reported $28.2 million in the third quarter alone, nearly 200,000 donors and an average gift of $39.63, https://www.sherrodbrown.com/2026/10/15/as-record-spending-from-corporate-special-interests-bail-out-husted-sherrod-brown-raises-over-28-2-million-in-q3/. He is also the most heavily targeted: VoteLog shows $164,312,026 in outside spending against him, against $108,391,198 against Husted.

Interests with money and a live decision:

1. **Crypto.** Fairshake's affiliate Defend American Jobs reported $11.4 million against Brown in its first filing on September 24, 2026, part of a $30 million pledge, https://cointelegraph.com/news/crypto-industry-spending-ohio-senate-race-sherrod-brown. VoteLog now shows $55,760,700 from that group. Brown chaired Senate Banking and was a leading crypto critic. The decision is crypto market structure law in 2027.
2. **The SMP network.** WinSenate at $62,524,530, Duty And Country, Lower Costs Now and Defend The Vote carry his outside side. SMP funds three of them.
3. **Environmental groups and utilities.** LCV, NRDC Action Fund and EDF Action launched a $9 million campaign tying Husted to the FirstEnergy scandal, https://www.eenews.net/articles/greens-take-on-husted-in-9m-ohio-senate-race-ad-campaign/. Brown wants data centers to pay their full power costs.

## Committee structure and treasurer

The campaign committee is Friends of Sherrod Brown, with campaign manager Patrick Eisenhauer. The third quarter release splits $23.1 million to the campaign and $5.1 million to affiliated fundraising committees that are not named. VoteLog shows $9,159,247 in transfers from authorized committees. The FEC committee ID and treasurer were not verified.

## Small donors

Documented. He raised $14.3 million in 2025 and held nearly $9.9 million at year end, per NOTUS through Signal Ohio, https://signalohio.org/sherrod-brown-eclipsed-jon-husted-fundraising-ohio-election-2026/. Third quarter 2026: $28.2 million total, nearly 60,000 first time donors, 95 percent of gifts $100 or less. October 1 to 14: $6.7 million, per CQ Roll Call, https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/oct/23/senate-democratic-candidates-keep-the-cash-flowing/.

Inference: Like Acton on the same ballot, Brown has no single dominant donor. The asks come from institutions spending for and against him.

## Crypto and finance

Documented. Fairshake spent over $40 million against Brown in 2024 and raised $190 million ahead of 2026, per Signal Ohio. Cointelegraph names Coinbase and Ripple Labs as backers without amounts. VoteLog shows Fairshake giving Defend American Jobs $106,000,000 across all races. Husted spokesperson Amy Natoce said Brown faced an onslaught of ads. Brown said in August 2025 that his goal was for crypto to expand opportunity and lift up Ohioans.

Inference: Crypto money is buying a no vote on Brown rather than a yes on Husted. If Brown wins, his posture on market structure legislation is the one decision the industry has the most riding on.

## Party and leadership

Documented. VoteLog lists SMP giving $171,120,000 to WinSenate, $17,500,000 to Duty And Country and $11,670,000 to Defend The Vote across all races. The Brown campaign banner cites another outlet saying Republicans outspent him two to one in one week; that figure is not stored.

## Utilities, data centers and environmental groups

Documented. LCV Victory Fund counts $5,317,023 for Brown, and LCV Inc. gave it $18,020,601 this cycle. The trio's $9 million issue campaign says the FirstEnergy scheme happened on Husted's watch. Brown pledges that data centers pay full electricity costs and wants their tax breaks ended, https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/2026/08/10/ohio-u-s-senate-candidates-offer-contrasting-visions-for-data-centers/. FactCheck.org found his ads' individual statements mostly accurate but said they imply Husted was accused of wrongdoing, which he was not, and that HB 6 does not explain most of the bill increase, https://www.factcheck.org/2026/09/ohio-democratic-ads-connect-husted-to-bribery-scandal-despite-no-charges-of-wrongdoing/.

## Own finances and disclosure history

No disclosure was retrieved in this pass. He served six House terms and three Senate terms and lost in 2024 to Bernie Moreno by 3.6 points, https://www.springfieldnewssun.com/local/brown-husted-make-pitches-on-affordability-data-centers-and-more-in-u-s-senate-race/article_33e17de8-63b2-54dd-8ce7-5acffd32da7d.html.

## Enforcement and controversies

- No FEC or ethics matter was found in sources read.
- FactCheck.org rated the FirstEnergy ads misleading by juxtaposition. Husted's spokesperson called them baseless lies. Brown's campaign response to the fact check was not found.
- An outside attack ad accuses Brown on transgender youth care; the sponsor was not identified, https://www.springfieldnewssun.com/local/brown-husted-make-pitches-on-affordability-data-centers-and-more-in-u-s-senate-race/article_33e17de8-63b2-54dd-8ce7-5acffd32da7d.html.
