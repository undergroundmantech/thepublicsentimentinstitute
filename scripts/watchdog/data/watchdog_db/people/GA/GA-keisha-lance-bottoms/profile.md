# Keisha Lance Bottoms Watchdog File

Subject: Keisha Lance Bottoms, Democratic nominee for Governor of Georgia, 2026
Compiled: October 23, 2026 for the On Point Politics watchdog database, onpointpolitics.us
Data cutoff: October 22, 2026. The latest filed Georgia figures run through July 31, 2026. Later figures are campaign announcements.
Next scheduled data drop: the Georgia report covering activity through October 20, 2026, due the week of October 26 according to the AJC. The exact due date was not verified.

## Bottom line up front

1. The Democratic Governors Association is her financial base. It gave more than $1.6 million to her leadership committee in June and July, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/08/georgia-governor-race-rick-jackson-raises-13m-in-outside-money/). Her campaign then said the DGA gave $11.5 million of the $23.2 million she raised from August 1 to October 20, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/keisha-lance-bottoms-fundraising/). The October figures are provisional until the filing lands.
2. Billionaire Democrats arrived late. JB Pritzker gave $1 million to her leadership fund after the October 20 cutoff, and Michael Bloomberg gave at least $1 million, per the [AJC on October 21](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/pritzker-bloomberg-donate-keisha-lance-bottoms-georgia-governor/).
3. Her signature policy fights put her against large industries: a statewide pause on new data center construction, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/business/2026/10/poll-georgians-government-regulation-of-ai/); Medicaid expansion, per her [AJC column](https://www.ajc.com/opinion/2026/10/keisha-lance-bottoms-affordability-georgia/); and ending the 2.25 percent auto insurance premium tax, per [FOX 5](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/keisha-lance-bottoms-georgia-governor-candidate-profile). No donors from those industries were documented on either side.
4. Real estate is a small but traceable bloc, at least $30,000 from commercial real estate figures through July, per [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/atlanta/economic-development/jackson-far-outraising-bottoms-georgia-governor-race-cre-execs-donations-campaign-finance). Her record as mayor includes a long term downtown redevelopment deal.
5. Disclosure history is the opening for critics: a $37,000 state ethics fine in 2021 for her 2017 race, a $125 late filing fine in 2026, and paid property tax liens reported by the AJC in October.

## Committee structure and treasurer

Bottoms runs a capped Georgia candidate committee, opened in spring 2025, and Project Battleground Georgia, a leadership committee she created after winning the May 19, 2026 primary outright, per the [Georgia Recorder](https://cobbcountycourier.com/2026/08/gop-candidates-outraise-democrats-so-far-in-most-but-not-all-of-georgias-statewide-contests/). Georgia's 2021 law lets the two parties' nominees for governor run leadership committees that take unlimited gifts. Project Battleground Georgia had raised more than $3 million by July 31 and at least $3.4 million by mid September, per the AJC. Her campaign committee raised $5.7 million from May 6, 2025 through July 31, 2026 and held just over $770,000 at the end of July, per [Capitol Beat](https://roughdraftatlanta.com/2026/08/11/rick-jackson-outpaces-bottoms-fundraising/). The AJC put cash on hand at about $1.3 million for the same date, a difference likely explained by counting both accounts.

The treasurer, the state filer IDs and any compliance firm were not found because the state commission site refused automated access. Communications director TaNisha Cameron speaks for the campaign. Bottoms lent her campaign $200,000 in its first half year, per [FOX 5](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/keisha-lance-bottoms-jason-esteves-fundraising-georgia-governor-race), a campaign announcement made before the filing.

## Party and leadership: the DGA

Documented. DGA money reached Project Battleground Georgia in the June 11 to July 31 period. The AJC reported more than $1.6 million and the Georgia Recorder about $1.8 million; the outlets differ. For August 1 to October 20 her campaign reported $11.5 million from the DGA, the largest single source, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/keisha-lance-bottoms-fundraising/). The DGA also pledged to spend at least $10 million for her and said "Republicans are drastically outspending us." Sen. Jon Ossoff said he moved more than $3.6 million over two years to the Democratic Party of Georgia's coordinated campaign, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/09/can-keisha-lance-bottoms-withstand-rick-jacksons-spending-blitz-in-georgia/). That money supports the ticket and is not stored as a gift to Bottoms.

What they want and the lever. The party's ask is the seat itself: the first Democratic governor since Roy Barnes, per the AJC. The governor's veto, budget and appointments are the lever.

Inference: by her own numbers the DGA supplied about half of her late money, so the national party has more invested in her than any Georgia donor documented here.

## Billionaire megadonors

Documented. Pritzker gave $1 million to the leadership fund, and Bloomberg gave at least $1 million, which her campaign confirmed; the AJC cited Politico and the New York Times, which were not read. Pritzker also gave $100,000 to Oregon Gov. Tina Kotek this month, per a [Willamette Week](https://www.wweek.com/news/2026/10/13/jb-pritzker-gives-100k-to-kotek/) headline only.

The lever. Neither donor has a documented Georgia business ask in the sources read. Bloomberg's known causes and Pritzker's are not stored here without a source.

Inference: these look like party building gifts to a toss up race, not bloc purchases. They should be watched on the filing for the account and date.

## Real estate and development

Documented. Through July, commercial real estate donors gave Bottoms at least $30,000, per [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/atlanta/economic-development/jackson-far-outraising-bottoms-georgia-governor-race-cre-execs-donations-campaign-finance): Ronald Abramson of Buchanan $31,400, Gerald Alley of Con-Real $8,400 in June, Rod Mullice of Windsor Stevens Holdings more than $5,000, and Egbert Perry of Integral Group $2,500. Perry also gave Jackson $2,000. Avison Young's Kirk Rich hosted a fundraiser. Six figure checks to her leadership committee came from Sycamore Management at $100,000 and Matthew Bernstein at $50,000, per the AJC; their businesses were not resolved. Education Reform Now PAC and EMILY's List gave $25,000 each.

The lever. She proposes a statewide affordable housing trust fund and a higher homestead exemption. As Recreation Authority director she helped broker the Turner Field sale, and as mayor she helped negotiate a long term downtown redevelopment, per [FOX 5](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/keisha-lance-bottoms-georgia-governor-candidate-profile) and the [AP](https://newspressnow.com/?p=57692).

Inference: the dollar amounts are small. The bloc matters more as a relationship network from her Atlanta years than as a funding source.

## Own finances and disclosure history

No net worth estimate or state financial disclosure was found. Details about a family member who is not a political actor are left out. The AJC reported in October 2026 that more than a dozen property tax and HOA liens totaling over $47,000 since 2010 were recorded on household properties and that all were paid, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/keisha-lance-bottoms-and-her-husband-have-a-history-of-late-tax-payments/). Her campaign said she paid her taxes and pointed to Jackson's IRS dispute.

## Enforcement and controversies

2017 mayoral campaign. In 2021 her campaign agreed to a $37,000 state ethics fine. The commission's director called some of the violations procedural. No direct response from Bottoms was found.

2026 late filing. Her July 31 report came in a day after the grace period and carries a $125 fine. The campaign blamed a technical error, per the Georgia Recorder.

2021 refunds. Her mayoral account reported more than $413,000 in spending including refunds of 238 donations after she declined to run again, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/news/atlanta-news/atlanta-mayor-returns-413000-in-campaign-refunds/WAHEOKFRLRAT5J4SWYGWTLVJEM/). No finding of wrongdoing was reported.

Record as mayor. Jackson's ads attack her public safety record. An AJC analysis found Atlanta homicides rose 59 percent from 2019 to 2020. Bottoms cites the pandemic and points to police raises and the training center. A federal corruption probe of her predecessor's administration overshadowed her first term, per the AP. She is not reported as a target.

National Guard. She said Jackson promised to send the Guard during the election. Jackson called it "a complete falsehood." The claim is stored as contested.
