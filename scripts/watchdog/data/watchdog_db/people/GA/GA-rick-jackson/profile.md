# Rick Jackson Watchdog File

Subject: Rick Jackson, Republican nominee for Governor of Georgia, 2026
Compiled: October 23, 2026 for the On Point Politics watchdog database, onpointpolitics.us

Next scheduled data drop: the Georgia report covering activity through October 20, 2026, due the week of October 26 according to the AJC. The exact due date was not verified.

## Bottom line up front

1. Rick Jackson is mostly his own donor. He spent more than $108 million of his own fortune to win the nomination through June 2026, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/07/heres-how-rick-jackson-spent-108-million-in-the-republican-race-for-governor/), and his campaign accounts had raised roughly $130 million through July 31, much of it his own money, per the [AJC on October 21](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/pritzker-bloomberg-donate-keisha-lance-bottoms-georgia-governor/). Analysts expect about $200 million by Election Day. That is an estimate, not a filing.
2. His own company is the largest interest in the file. Jackson Healthcare subsidiaries have received more than $1 billion in payments from Georgia agencies since 2020 and more than $56 million in 2025 alone from Behavioral Health, Community Health and Corrections, per the [AJC on March 5, 2026](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/03/rick-jackson-promises-to-unwind-his-companys-state-contracts-if-elected/). The governor appoints the people who run those agencies.
3. The Republican Governors Association gave $10 million to his new leadership committee, For All Georgians Inc., about two weeks after he opened it in June 2026, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/08/georgia-governor-race-rick-jackson-raises-13m-in-outside-money/) and the [Georgia Recorder](https://cobbcountycourier.com/2026/08/gop-candidates-outraise-democrats-so-far-in-most-but-not-all-of-georgias-statewide-contests/). Months earlier he had sued to stop his primary rival from using the same kind of committee.
4. A live federal tax fight. The IRS says about $54 million in Jackson deductions tied to film investments were improper and assessed $16 million in penalties. Jackson is disputing both in U.S. Tax Court, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/10/keisha-lance-bottoms-and-her-husband-have-a-history-of-late-tax-payments/). He has no public position on the Georgia film tax credit in the sources read.
5. Trump world. He gave $1 million to MAGA Inc. about a month before launching in February 2026, per [CNN via ABC17](https://abc17news.com/?p=1624327), and has promised to be Trump's "favorite governor."

## Committee structure and treasurer

Jackson runs a capped Georgia candidate committee and an unlimited leadership committee, For All Georgians Inc., which he opened after winning the June 16, 2026 runoff over Lt. Gov. Burt Jones. Georgia's 2021 law lets the governor, the lieutenant governor, the two parties' nominees for those offices and legislative leaders run leadership committees that take unlimited gifts and coordinate with campaigns, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/02/how-georgias-unlimited-money-machine-is-powering-the-governors-race/) and [GPB](https://www.gpb.org/news/2026/02/24/judge-rules-against-burt-jones-unlimited-fundraising-in-race-for-georgia-governor). For All Georgians transferred $5 million to the campaign committee in the June 11 to July 31 period, per [Capitol Beat](https://roughdraftatlanta.com/2026/08/11/rick-jackson-outpaces-bottoms-fundraising/). The campaign reported $4.3 million cash at the end of July, while the AJC put cash across all his accounts at roughly $11 million.

The treasurer, the state filer IDs and any compliance firm were not found. The Georgia Government Transparency and Campaign Finance Commission site refused automated access, so no filing was read directly. Spokespeople on record include Garrison Douglas, Dave Abrams and Mike Schrimpf. Schrimpf was also paid $90,000 in consulting fees.

## Self funding

Documented. Jackson pledged at least $50 million at his February 4, 2026 launch, per the [AJC](https://feeds.ajc.com/politics/2026/02/republican-outsiders-50m-bet-remakes-georgia-governors-race). By July 2 he had given more than $111 million and his campaign had spent more than $108 million, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/07/heres-how-rick-jackson-spent-108-million-in-the-republican-race-for-governor/). After the nomination he put in no new cash or loans through July 31, only tens of thousands in kind for legal fees, rent and travel, per [Capitol Beat](https://roughdraftatlanta.com/2026/08/11/rick-jackson-outpaces-bottoms-fundraising/). AdImpact counted $102 million in ad reservations this year and an $87 million general election buy, per the AJC. His ad line: "I'll spend whatever it takes to win this race." He told WJCL "I can't be bought."

Money out through July 2: Smart Media Group of Alexandria, Virginia, more than $62 million in TV and cable plus $750,000 radio; Ballast, more than $18 million; GMRP, roughly $14 million, mostly mail; Quiver, roughly $5.7 million; polling about $2.7 million to unnamed vendors; legal about $500,000, mostly Kilpatrick Townsend & Stockton; Crosby Ottenhoff Group about $200,000; AJ Strategies more than $125,000; Michael Schrimpf $90,000; Robinson Republic about $50,000. Staff salaries were only about $209,000.

Inference: a candidate who funds himself owes few donors, which is his own argument. The flip side is that the one interest with the most money riding on the governor's office is his own company, covered next.

## Health care and pharma: Jackson Healthcare and the state

Documented. Jackson owns and runs Jackson Healthcare of Alpharetta, which reports more than $3 billion a year in income. Its subsidiaries Healthcare Workforce Logistics, Jackson & Coker and LocumTenens have taken more than $1 billion in Georgia agency payments since a 2020 COVID staffing contract that was awarded without bids under the public health emergency, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/03/rick-jackson-promises-to-unwind-his-companys-state-contracts-if-elected/). In 2025 the payments topped $56 million from Behavioral Health, Community Health and Corrections. He also bought a Tennessee antibiotics plant in 2021, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/02/why-rick-jackson-says-hell-be-trumps-favorite-governor/).

The lever. The governor appoints the commissioners of those three departments. Community Health runs Medicaid. A bill to bar people with state business from statewide office stalled when Speaker Jon Burns declined to call it in March 2026, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/03/6-takeaways-from-a-hectic-crossover-day-in-the-georgia-legislature/).

What he has said. His campaign said on March 5 that the company "will not bid on any new contracts with the state of Georgia" and will "work to responsibly unwind any existing contracts." Jackson said "Whatever the law says we're going to do, we're going to do." His campaign says he has pledged to give 100 percent of his net worth to charity. No blind trust or divestiture pledge was found. Jones called the company the "culprit" in rising costs; the Jackson camp called that a "meltdown." Jackson opposes Medicaid expansion and backs a "state solution" to cut middlemen.

Inference: "unwind" has no published timetable, no named contracts and no independent monitor. Whoever runs Behavioral Health, Community Health and Corrections in 2027 will be a Jackson appointee deciding whether and how fast his company's state revenue winds down.

## Party and leadership: the RGA and For All Georgians

Documented. The RGA gave $10 million to For All Georgians in June 2026. Other six figure checks to the committee were $100,000 each from Ben Tarbutton, Evans General Contractors and Curae Finance, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/08/georgia-governor-race-rick-jackson-raises-13m-in-outside-money/). In February Jackson sued Jones, Attorney General Chris Carr and Ethics Commission members in federal court, arguing the leadership committee law gave Jones "his own rulebook." Judge Thomas Thrash issued a restraining order on February 20, then ruled against Jones on March 13 but stayed the ruling, per [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/atlanta/news/gop-candidate-for-georgia-governor-wins-temporary-restraining-order-against-lt-gov-burt-jones-leadership-committee/) and the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/03/judge-lets-burt-jones-committee-continue-to-spend-on-his-campaign-for-now/).

Inference: Jackson attacked the unlimited committee as unfair when he could not have one and adopted it once he could. The RGA's own top funders were not traced in this pass.

## Trump world

Documented. Jackson gave $1 million to MAGA Inc. about a month before launching, per [CNN via ABC17](https://abc17news.com/?p=1624327), which Jones called an "11th hour MAGA embrace," per the AJC. The disclosure form itself was not read. The IRS film deduction dispute is pending in Tax Court. The case number and filing dates were not found.

## Enforcement and controversies

Georgians for Integrity, an anonymous Delaware nonprofit with an East Atlanta mail drop, spent about $20 million attacking Jones, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/06/rick-jackson-topples-burt-jones-wins-gop-nod-for-governor/). The Georgia GOP filed an ethics complaint seeking donor disclosure, per the [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/01/an-8m-mystery-triggers-growing-fight-over-dark-money-in-georgia-politics/). Jones allies suspected Jackson. Jackson has repeatedly denied any connection, and no filing links him. Status contested.

Tax Court. Allegation: the IRS says $54 million in film investment deductions were improper. Response: Jackson is contesting it in court. The Bottoms campaign says he "owes $54 million in federal taxes and $16 million in penalties," which overstates what the AJC reports.

Defamation suit. Jackson sued Jones in March 2026 over campaign statements. The Jones campaign said "Rick Jackson's thin skin is showing." No outcome was found.

National Guard. Bottoms said on October 6 that Jackson promised to send the National Guard during the election. Jackson called it "a complete falsehood," per [Atlanta News First](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/video/2026/10/07/bottoms-says-jackson-promises-send-national-guard-during-election/).
