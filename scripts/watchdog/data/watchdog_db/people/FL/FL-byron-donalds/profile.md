# PART 1: FIRST HOP

# Billionaires, Bookmakers and Jailers Bankroll Donalds

Byron Donalds has raised nearly $149 million for governor as of October 20, 2026, and roughly nine tenths of it runs through one unlimited state committee, Friends of Byron Donalds PAC, whose largest checks come from Ken Griffin at $10 million, Jeff Yass at about $11 million counting his pass-through to Club for Growth Action Florida, the Seminole Tribe at $5.5 million, the GEO Group at $2.5 million to $3.5 million, and Richard Uihlein at $2 million, per the [Tampa Bay Times](https://kdhnews.com/news/politics/18-billionaires-have-funded-the-florida-governor-race-17-gave-to-donalds/article_f4a68240-1521-537e-b8cb-9006156ebc14.html) and [Florida Phoenix](https://floridaphoenix.com/2026/10/20/democratic-governors-association-investing-1-million-to-support-david-jolly/). Seventeen billionaires gave him $23.3 million, about two thirds of his money came from corporations, political committees and donors giving $10,000 or more, and 30 percent came from outside Florida. The donors cluster into eleven identifiable blocs, and in nearly every one the record contains a policy ask Donalds has already answered: he opposes a utility return cap after NextEra and Duke gave $1.5 million, he pledges to keep the 2023 tort reforms that insurers and Americans for Prosperity want, he sponsored the detention bills GEO lobbied for before GEO wrote seven-figure checks, and he and his wife Erika run a household whose reported wealth is almost entirely her for-profit charter management firms, which would sit under the Department of Education he would oversee. The one claim the user should not make is that Jeff Yass funded Israel's Kohelet Policy Forum: Haaretz reported it in 2021, but the New York Times, the Washington Post and Calcalist all reported that Yass never donated and that Arthur Dantchik was the dominant funder who withdrew in August 2023. The personal finance file is a pattern of under-disclosure corrected only after press reports: a Campaign Legal Center complaint over 108 unreported trades, four years of omitted liabilities amended in June 2026, a Federal Election Commission deadlock on a 2020 soft money chain, a rebuffed FEC demand to refund roughly $400,000, and a bitcoin purchase on the same day he signed a letter asking the IRS to loosen crypto tax rules. The next data drop is the October 30, 2026 Florida disclosure deadline, which will settle the late September and October gifts that are currently reported only by secondary outlets.

## Two state vehicles and a treasurer who also serves Trump

Donalds runs a capped candidate account, Byron Donalds for Governor, and the unlimited affiliated committee Friends of Byron Donalds PAC, Florida Division of Elections account 89043, chaired by campaign chief strategist Ryan Smith with treasurer Bradley T. Crate of Red Curve Solutions in Beverly, Massachusetts, per the [Division of Elections](https://dos.elections.myflorida.com/committees/ComDetail.asp?account=89043). Crate is also treasurer of every federal Donalds committee: Byron Donalds for Congress, FEC ID C00733329; the leadership PAC Proven Conservatives PAC, C00755728; a second leadership PAC, Proven Conservatives PAC Federal, registered December 19, 2024; and the Byron Donalds Victory Fund joint fundraising committee whose participants include the NRCC and the Republican Party of Florida, per [FEC committee records](https://www.fec.gov/data/committee/C00760108/). Red Curve is the compliance shop used by Trump's campaign and MAGA Inc., so the treasurer is the clearest documented vendor link to Trump world.

The scale of the state committee dwarfs anything in his federal history. His House committee raised **$3.25 million in 2020, $5.66 million in 2022 and $6.60 million in 2024**, per [FEC candidate pages](https://www.fec.gov/data/candidate/H2FL14186/?cycle=2024&election_full=true). The state PAC alone had taken in nearly $130.4 million through the October 3 to 9, 2026 reporting week, per the [Bradenton Journal](https://thebradentonjournal.substack.com/p/donations-from-neal-beruff-add-to), and combined totals reached **$134.4 million raised and $91.7 million spent by September 25**, per [CBS Miami](https://www.cbsnews.com/miami/news/byron-donalds-new-ads-florida-governor-race-david-jolly/). Through May 31, 2026 the split was $71.8 million to the PAC against $9.2 million to the candidate account across 35,292 donors, per [Floridian Press](https://floridianpress.com/2026/06/byron-donalds-adds-13-8-million-to-gubernatorial-war-chest-brings-total-to-over-81-million/). Because Florida allows unlimited gifts to a candidate's affiliated committee, most of what would be outside super PAC money in a federal race flows directly into Friends of Byron Donalds, which means federal-style independent expenditure reporting badly understates the picture. David Jolly, by comparison, had raised under $22 million by October 20, with Donald Sussman at $600,000 and Charles Wall at $525,000 as his largest donors, per [WUSF](https://wusf.org/text/politics-issues/2026-09-09/what-to-know-about-pacs-your-florida-breaks-it-down).

The rest of this report is organized as a money map: one section per interest bloc, each naming the donors, the amounts and dates the notes could document, what that bloc wants from Tallahassee, and what Donalds has done or pledged that aligns. Within each section, documented facts are separated from inference.

## The Kohelet question: what can be said accurately

The user should treat this point as a landmine and state it precisely. Kohelet Policy Forum, founded in January 2012 by Moshe Koppel, produced the policy papers behind Israel's 2023 judicial overhaul and reportedly wrote the legislation submitted by Yariv Levin and Simcha Rothman, per [PBS and AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/an-american-billionaire-says-hell-stop-funding-the-think-tank-behind-israels-controversial-judicial-overhaul) and [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohelet_Policy_Forum). In March 2021 Haaretz reported that both Jeff Yass and his Susquehanna co-founder Arthur Dantchik were behind a large portion of Kohelet's donations through opaque American vehicles, and The Intercept repeated that framing in February 2023 without amounts, per [The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2023/02/16/israel-jeff-yass-kohelet). That is the alleged version.

The better-sourced version is different. Calcalist reported on August 4, 2023, citing sources close to Dantchik, that **Yass "never donated" to Kohelet**, and JTA wrote that this "confirms New York Times and Washington Post reporting and contradicts the 2021 Haaretz investigation," per [Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/hkora4ci3) and [JTA](https://www.jta.org/2023/08/04/israel/wall-street-billionaire-ends-funding-for-think-tank-behind-netanyahus-judicial-overhaul-plan). The Washington Post's March 2023 source said Dantchik was not the sole funder but that Yass never gave, per [InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/person/jeffrey-yass/). Dantchik's giving is confirmed by his own statement that day: "I stopped donating to think tanks in Israel, including the Kohelet Policy Forum." A Shomrim investigation traced about $12 million in 2021 from Dantchik's Claws Foundation through Silver Valley Research Foundation to the Central Fund of Israel, which was Kohelet's sole listed donor that year, per [Shomrim](https://www.shomrim.news/eng/danchik-kohelet). Calcalist's reporting put Dantchik at roughly 93 percent of Kohelet's budget, per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohelet_Policy_Forum). After the withdrawal, Kohelet cut about half its staff in April 2024 and its donations fell from 30 million shekels in 2022 to 13.6 million in 2024, per [Israel Hayom](https://www.israelhayom.com/2024/04/02/kohelet-policy-forum-scales-down-activity-due-to-funding-crunch/) and [Shomrim](https://www.shomrim.news/eng/funding-crisis-forces-kohelet-to-cut-salaries-research-and-support-for-other-groups).

The defensible public formulation is: Donalds' largest donor, Jeff Yass, was named by Haaretz in 2021 as a Kohelet funder alongside his business partner Arthur Dantchik; later reporting by the New York Times, Washington Post and Calcalist found Yass never donated, while Dantchik, who was the dominant funder, publicly stopped giving in August 2023. No filing shows a Yass or Susquehanna Foundation grant to American Friends of Kohelet, the Central Fund of Israel or Silver Valley. Yass does have documented indirect ties to pro-Israel giving: the Guardian found the Claws Foundation, where he served as a director, gave $16 million to pro-Israel and anti-Muslim groups, per the [Daily Beast summary](https://blogs.thedailybeast.com/gop-megadonor-jeff-yass-poured-dollar16m-into-anti-muslim-groups-report). That is a different claim from funding Kohelet and should not be blended with it.

## School choice and the Donalds family businesses

Documented. The school choice bloc is the one where donor interest, candidate policy and household income converge. Yass's $7.5 million and Griffin's $10 million both come from avowed school choice backers, Erika Donalds met the Yasses after OptimaEd was a 2023 Yass Prize semifinalist, and Donalds co-sponsored the 2017 Schools of Hope law and the charter co-location law and chaired the Florida House PreK-12 Quality Subcommittee, per [Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/the-billionaire-trying-to-reinvent-florida-schools-has-a-7-5-million-bet-on-byron-donalds-40565603/) and the [Miami Herald](https://www.aol.com/articles/byron-donalds-runs-governor-wife-150251717.html). AFP Action, which endorsed him March 4, 2026, credits him with the legislation that created Florida's Hope Scholarship, per [AFP Action](https://afpaction.com/afp-action-endorses-byron-donalds-for-governor/). Turning Point Action endorsed him January 13, 2026 citing school choice and parental rights. Charles "Chip" Cordes, co-founder of Summit Construction, the firm that built the Optima schools, gave $8,400 to Donalds' House campaign in 2020, matched by his wife, plus $2,000 in 2021, per [Florida Bulldog](https://www.floridabulldog.org/2026/01/charter-business-owned-rep-donalds-wife-cut-side-deals-construction-firm-suit-says/).

What this bloc wants. Florida's universal voucher program costs about $4.5 billion a year for more than 500,000 students at roughly $8,000 each, and the Florida Education Association sued over it in May 2026, per [Florida Trib](https://floridatrib.org/2026/07/30/what-byron-and-erika-donalds-gospel-of-school-choice-could-mean-for-florida/). The ask is to protect and expand that program and to open it further to private operators and ed-tech vendors.

What Donalds has pledged. His plan puts parents "at the head of the table," replaces A to F school grades and annual testing with individualized learning plans, and treats parents as consumers and schools as competitors with a heavy AI and ed-tech role. Erika Donalds said in October 2026 that vouchers should be funded from the same pot as public and charter schools, per [WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2026-10-19/erika-donalds-weighs-in-on-immigration-school-vouchers-state-power-over-local-governments), and said in March 2026 that "profit is not going to be a bad word when it comes to the education industry." Donalds' response is that "no donor has asked me to pursue school choice as governor because they don't have to." The Optima money flows that make this a conflict question are laid out in their own section below.

Inference. This is the bloc where the governor's office has the most direct levers: the Department of Education, the State Board of Education and the voucher administration all sit under the executive. No source documents a specific Donalds vote that benefited Optima, and Florida Bulldog says the same, but the structural overlap between the household's income and the office's authority is unlike any other bloc here.

## Gambling and prediction markets: more than $6 million ahead of a compact fight

Documented. What this bloc wants. The 2021 compact's exclusivity delivers about $1 billion a year to the state. The Tribe and the sportsbooks are on opposite sides of that fight and both are funding him.

What Donalds has done. He has taken no public position on prediction markets or the compact. Kalshi, a donor, ran a market showing him above 95 percent to win the primary, which he promoted on social media, per [Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/the-tiktok-billionaire-bankrolling-byron-donalds-40558946/).

Inference. Both sides buying access before a 2027 compact fight is the most plausible reading. The Tribe's August 2025 $1 million was described by Florida Politics as possibly hedging against a Casey DeSantis or Collins run, which suggests the Tribe is paying for a seat regardless of nominee rather than for a specific promise.

## Erika Donalds' Optima entities and the conflict a governor cannot recuse from

The structure. No reporting on Dewalt was found. She leads education policy at the America First Policy Institute, sits on a DeSantis-appointed FGCU trustee seat, and was a Heritage Foundation visiting fellow.

The dollar flows. Auditor filings show the Foundation received management fees of **$4,303,822 from Treasure Coast Classical, $8,424,572 from Naples Classical and $2,398,221 from Jacksonville Classical**, roughly $15.1 million; Optima-run schools paid Educator Solutions, a payroll firm founded by state Representative John Snyder that paid Erika more than $500,000 in salary, $6,930,584 in 2020 to 2022; the two for-profits took in **$5,843,969 in 2023 alone**; Naples Classical's 12 percent fee rose from $1.24 million to the Foundation in 2021 to 2022 to $1.56 million to Optima Management Services in 2023 to 2024; and the virtual Optima Classical Academy paid OptimaEd $1,085,266 in 2023 and $2.1 million in 2024, nearly **89 percent of the $3,588,660 in government funds** it received, per [Florida Bulldog](https://www.floridabulldog.org/2025/06/firms-belonging-to-rep-donalds-wife-grabbed-millions-in-charter-school-contracts/). CBS News found that between 2020 and 2023 the schools spent roughly 30 percent of their government funding, about **$35 million**, on outside firms tied to Erika, and a University of Miami charter expert found students at the four opened schools performed below average in math and reading, per [CBS via Ravitch](https://dianeravitch.net/2025/12/04/florida-wife-of-gop-gubernatorial-candidate-builds-a-for-profit-charter-school-empire). Summit Construction Management won $14.4 million, $20 million, $1 million and $11 million in building contracts for the schools, per [Florida Bulldog](https://www.floridabulldog.org/2026/01/charter-business-owned-rep-donalds-wife-cut-side-deals-construction-firm-suit-says/). The flows overlap and are not additive, but they are consistent with the CBS figure.

The breakups and lawsuits. Treasure Coast Classical terminated its Foundation contract December 31, 2023 and both Jacksonville academies ended ties in 2023 citing auditor deficiencies in debts, textbooks, revenues and vendor invoices. All four opened schools have cut ties. Treasure Coast Classical sued in late 2024, alleging Optima subcontracted all its employees to OptimaEd over a board veto, steered no-bid contracts to Summit, paid a $127,487.98 Summit invoice without authorization and failed to disclose that Summit-related entities had invested in Optima-related entities; a Martin County judge dismissed on venue grounds in May 2025 and the school appealed, per [Florida Bulldog](https://www.floridabulldog.org/2026/01/charter-business-owned-rep-donalds-wife-cut-side-deals-construction-firm-suit-says/). Paul Renner proposed a ban on first-time government contracts to officials and spouses and three-day trade disclosure in direct response, and Jay Collins' April 2026 opposition document led with the $35 million figure.

The conflict question. Erika says she does no business with the state of Florida and that there is "no conflict," and the campaign calls her work a longstanding career, per [Florida Trib](https://floridatrib.org/2026/07/30/what-byron-and-erika-donalds-gospel-of-school-choice-could-mean-for-florida/). The accurate framing is narrower than "state contracts" and more structural: her firms are paid by charter schools and a virtual academy whose revenue is state per-pupil funding and taxpayer scholarships, her Education Freedom Foundation sells consulting to charter founders in ESA states, and she publicly advocates funding vouchers from the same pot as public schools. A governor appoints the Commissioner of Education through the State Board he appoints, signs the education budget, and oversees the scholarship funding organizations. Unlike a legislator, he cannot recuse from the Department of Education. ## Gambling and prediction markets: the Tribe has already fired, and the governor must pick a side

Documented. The 2021 compact runs through July 31, 2051 and grants the Seminole Tribe exclusivity over slots, craps, roulette, banked card games, fantasy contests and statewide mobile sports betting through a hub-and-spoke server model, with the state guaranteed at least $2.5 billion over the first five years, per the [Florida Senate summary](https://www.flsenate.gov/PublishedContent/Offices/2020-2022/President/Documents/Summaryof2021Compact.pdf). The state's revenue estimators recorded **$817.3 million in revenue share in FY 2024 to 2025**, forecast **$903.3 million for FY 2025 to 2026**, and project more than $1 billion a year from FY 2028 to 2029, roughly $6.1 billion over the forecast period, per the [EDR Indian Gaming conference of Aug 4, 2025](https://edr.state.fl.us/Content/conferences/Indian-gaming/archives/250804IndianGaming.pdf). The compact's only built-in reopener was a 36-month good-faith negotiation window on online casino gaming, which closed in late 2024 without an amendment, per the same Senate summary. The governor executes compacts and must cooperate in seeking Interior approval, and compacts take effect only after legislative ratification, per [Fla. Stat. 285.710](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xix-public-business/fl-st-sect-285-710/). The governor also appoints all five members of the Florida Gaming Control Commission, subject to Senate confirmation, for four-year terms, and is barred from soliciting recommendations from anyone tied to a tribe's gaming operation, per [Fla. Stat. 16.71](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/16.71).

On October 8, 2026 the Tribe filed a 72-page complaint in Broward Circuit Court against DraftKings, CEO Jason Robins and GUS III LLC, the operator of DraftKings Predictions, seeking an injunction and disgorgement of all Florida revenue and a civil racketeering finding against Robins personally; the complaint alleges that on August 28, 2026 DraftKings replaced American odds with cent prices for Florida users and that "DraftKings changed the label on the price. It did not change the price, the product, or the transaction," per [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/281066/draftkings-sued-by-seminole-tribe-over-sports-predictions-in-florida/). The same complaint alleges DraftKings secretly financed the West Flagler challenge and funded Protect the Constitution LLC, whose Amendment 3 suit Judge Jonathan Sjostrom dismissed for lack of standing on November 6, 2025 and which DraftKings joined as sole member on September 17, 2026; Attorney General James Uthmeier's office had urged dismissal in June 2025, per [CasinoBeats](https://casinobeats.com/2025/11/06/florida-judge-dismisses-protect-the-constitution-lawsuit-seminole-sports-betting/) and Legal Sports Report. Kalshi remains legal and available in Florida as of October 2026 with no state action against it, and HB 189, which would have made non-compact online wagering a third-degree felony, did not pass, per [Covers](https://www.covers.com/betting/prediction-sites/usa/florida-kalshi). Florida is one of six states that did not sign the attorneys general letter asserting state control over gambling, and Uthmeier "has taken a passive approach on prediction markets but has defended the tribe's compact," per [The Lines](https://www.thelines.com/legal-betting/seminole-tribe-spending-to-protect-florida-gambling-interests/). DeSantis raised the question publicly in February 2026, asking on CNBC whether "something like Kalshi" clashes with Florida law, per [Sportsbook Review](https://www.sportsbookreview.com/news/florida-governor-desantis-questions-kalshi-feb-25-2026/). The national map tightened in the fall: the Sixth Circuit held on September 25, 2026 that Kalshi had not shown its sports contracts are swaps under exclusive CFTC jurisdiction, the Ninth Circuit found two California tribes likely to succeed on IGRA claims, and 39 state attorneys general plus 130 tribes urged the Supreme Court to hear New Jersey's petition, per [Pillsbury](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/indian-gaming-prediction-markets-scotus.html) and [CDC Gaming](https://cdcgaming.com/brief/tribe-39-state-attorneys-general-latest-to-ask-supreme-court-to-hear-prediction-markets-case/).

Inference. The money from both sides is a purchase of a seat at a renegotiation only the governor can convene. ## Conclusion

The second hop changes the picture in one important way: the blocs are not independent. The attorney general, who shares the Seminole Tribe, GEO, CDR and Kalshi as donors with the governor, decides whether Florida sues prediction markets, defends the compact and enforces any AI statute; the PSC that sets utility returns also decides who pays for the data centers the AI investors want built; the emergency fund that pays detention vendors is the same fund that pays for hurricanes; and the House Speaker whose committees gave $1 million wrote the property tax amendment the Realtors are spending $18 million to pass. A Donalds administration would therefore be less a series of favors to donors than a single governing coalition, Perez's House plus Uthmeier's office plus a Koch-trained executive staff, that has already set its 2027 agenda: Amendment 3 implementation and a follow-on cut, no ROE cap, no data center moratorium, no voucher controls, continued detention funding, and silence on the compact until a court forces the question.

The decision that will reveal the most is the one he has refused to discuss. Every other bloc got a stated position before Election Day. On gambling he took $5.5 million from the Tribe and more than $1 million from the prediction markets and sportsbooks suing or being sued by it, and said nothing. Whether his first FGCC appointment, his first amicus decision after the Supreme Court acts, and his response to the Broward case favor the Tribe or the exchanges will show whether the money bought access or bought an outcome, and it is a decision with a nine-figure annual revenue line attached to it.
