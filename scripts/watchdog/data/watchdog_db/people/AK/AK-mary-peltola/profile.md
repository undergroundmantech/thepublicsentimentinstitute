# Mary Peltola Watchdog File

**Subject:** Mary Peltola, Democrat, candidate for U.S. Senate in Alaska, 2026, former U.S. Representative
**Compiled:** October 25, 2026 for the On Point Politics watchdog database, onpointpolitics.us
**Data cutoff:** October 24, 2026. The latest filing figures read run through July 29, 2026; a third quarter total was reported from the campaign.
**Next scheduled data drop:** FEC pre-general report covering through October 14, 2026, not read in this pass, then the post-general report due December 3, 2026. Alaska uses a top four primary and a ranked choice general.

## Bottom line up front

Peltola is the best funded challenger in this batch and one of the poorest candidates. Her principal committee took in $18.55 million through July 29, 2026, $16.26 million of it from individuals, per Legis1 on August 19, 2026, https://legis1.com/news/sullivan-peltola-campaign-donors-follow-the-money-1. The campaign then reported $17 million for July through September, relayed by Congress.net on October 13, 2026, https://congress.net/?p=9621. She reports assets as low as $47,000.

Three interests combine money with a decision she would touch:

1. **National Democratic leadership.** Senate Majority PAC reserved $10 million in May and more than $12 million by October. Most of her PAC money came from Democratic leadership PACs. The decision is the Senate majority.
2. **Small donors.** Nearly $3.5 million of her first quarter came in unitemized gifts, mostly through ActBlue, and she has more Alaska donors than Sullivan.
3. **Resource development through her former employer.** She earned $342,000 at Holland & Hart, whose lobbying clients include mining and energy companies. She calls herself pro-development. The decision is Arctic leasing and federal permitting.

## Committee structure and treasurer

The committee name, FEC ID and treasurer were not verified, and the roster has no FEC ID for her. No leadership PAC or joint fundraising committee was found. Spending is heavy on digital fundraising, about $1.4 million in the first quarter and $1.3 million in the second, per Alaska Public Media on April 28, 2026, https://www.ktoo.org/?p=350805, and the Anchorage Daily News on July 20, 2026, https://adn.com/politics/2026/07/20/peltola-raises-millions-more-than-sullivan-in-alaskas-us-senate-race.

Scale. Nearly $9 million in the first quarter, $6.9 million in the second, and $17 million reported for the third. Cash was $4.64 million around July 29, against Sullivan's $7.30 million.

## Party and leadership

Documented. Senate Majority PAC reserved $10 million in Alaska TV time on May 13, 2026 to run against Sullivan on costs and Trump loyalty, per Alaska Public Media, https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/washington-d-c/2026-05-13/democratic-group-plans-to-spend-10m-on-tv-ads-in-alaskas-u-s-senate-race. Congress.net put Senate Majority PAC reservations above $12 million and the Senate Leadership Fund at $17 million for Sullivan as of October 13. PACs gave her about $450,000 in the first quarter, mostly Democratic leadership PACs, and EMILY's List passed through $44,000.

Inference: As with other Democratic challengers, the decisive money is spent by leadership committees rather than given to her campaign.

## Small donors

Documented. Unitemized gifts were nearly $3.5 million in the first quarter, against Sullivan's $90,000 through WinRed. Her itemized Alaska money exceeded $500,000, against his $135,000. In state money was still only about 13 percent of her itemized total.

Inference: Most of her money is national. Her Alaska donor lead is real but small in dollar terms.

## Mining and resources

Documented. After losing in 2024 she joined Holland & Hart, a law firm and lobbying practice, as senior director of Alaska affairs. Her former chief of staff Anton McParland joined as senior director of federal affairs. The firm's lobbying clients include several mining companies, energy companies and the City of Kotzebue, per KTOO on March 3, 2025, https://www.ktoo.org/?p=338075. Her candidate disclosure shows $342,000 from the firm and $95,000 from two fellowships for 2025 and part of 2026, per the Alaska Beacon on October 6, 2026, https://alaskabeacon.com/?p=29586. At an Alaska Oil and Gas Association forum on August 27 she called herself a champion of the oil industry, claimed credit for Willow, and said she worked to stop an Endangered Species Act listing for Chinook salmon, https://alaskabeacon.com/2026/08/28/sullivan-goes-on-attack-in-first-post-primary-faceoff-for-alaskas-u-s-senate-race/.

Inference: Her energy positions line up with her former firm's client base, but no filing shows a client paying her campaign.

## Own finances and disclosure history

Her campaign told Newsweek her net worth is between $62,006 and $230,000, and she reportedly owns no stocks. Her 2025 House disclosure lists a mortgage of $250,000 to $500,000 and $10,000 to $15,000 in credit card debt, per Newsweek on September 29, 2026, https://www.newsweek.com/sullivan-peltola-net-worth-compared-alaska-12498773. Her campaign salary is about $1,300 a week.

## Enforcement and controversies

- No FEC or ethics complaint against her was found in sources read.
- Lobbying. Sullivan said at the October 16 debate that she earned almost half a million dollars as a D.C. lobbyist. She said the work benefited Alaskans, per Fox News, https://www.foxnews.com/politics/personal-attacks-fly-sullivan-peltola-spar-corruption-character-controversy. The disclosure figure found is $342,000.
- Staff treatment. Sullivan cited a press report of staff mistreatment. She called it gossip and unfounded, and her campaign disputed specific claims. Status Contested.
- The namesake candidate. A Daniel J. Sullivan filed on May 29, 2026. Press release metadata named a writer reportedly linked to a PAC that backed her past races, and he denies any contact with Peltola. The Alaska Supreme Court kept him on the ballot on June 29, 2026, per Straight Arrow News, https://san.com/cc/both-dan-sullivans-can-appear-in-contested-alaska-senate-race-though-their-names-may-look-different/. No Peltola response was found.
